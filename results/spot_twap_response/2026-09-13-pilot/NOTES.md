Correction to the part 6 side-call reading (part 6b): most of the counted side changes are boundary artifacts. When the target stamp w+h lies in a new market, the strike is the TWAP stamped at the boundary, two seconds after the anchor, so the anchor's side against it flips whenever the TWAP is trending: 1,800 of 1,912 boundary instants at h=5. Within a single market the genuine figures are: h=5, 131 side changes in 7,591 instants, 121 shown by the ghost, 4 false alarms; h=10, 245 changes, 225 shown, 12 false alarms; h=30, 692 changes, 497 shown, 91 false alarms. Price errors are unchanged. Use the within-market rows; the part 6 totals overstate the crossing catch rate.

Receipt-clock ghost (part 6): forecasts issued at wall-clock instants every 60 s using only TWAP events and spot samples received by that instant (slot value = newest spot sample stamped at or before the slot and received by the instant; the same rule carries values through silent seconds and fills future slots). Graded against the TWAP event stamped w+h, where w is the latest received TWAP stamp. Median error 0.005 bps at 5 s, 0.027 at 10 s, 0.292 at 30 s, against 0.157, 0.310 and 0.879 bps for persistence; provider-clock part 5 gave 0.006, 0.031 and 0.311. Assumed (not yet received) slots averaged 1.94, 6.94 and 26.94 with maxima 9, 14 and 34 during feed stalls. The target TWAP arrived a median 4.6 s, 9.6 s and 29.6 s after the instant (10th to 90th percentile 3.8 to 5.1 s at h=5). Side changes against the Price to Beat caught: 1,893 of 1,931 at 5 s (41 false alarms), 1,933 of 1,976 at 10 s (111), 1,954 of 2,218 at 30 s (334). Spot receipt times are the retained same-second values, so this is a retained-history approximation of live availability.

Rolling ghost TWAP (part 5): at an instant whose latest TWAP stamp is w, the TWAP stamped w+h is the mean of the stamps [w+h-62, w+h-3]; 63-h of them are already known and the h-3 future ones are assumed equal to the current carried-forward spot. Graded against the TWAP actually stamped w+h at 9,199 instants (every 60 s of the pilot week, provider clock): median error 0.006 bps at 5 s, 0.031 at 10 s, 0.311 at 30 s, versus 0.155, 0.307 and 0.868 bps for assuming the TWAP stays put. Of the instants where the TWAP changed side against the Price to Beat within the horizon, the ghost already showed the new side in 1,021 of 1,052 at 5 s (24 false alarms), 1,071 of 1,158 at 10 s (74) and 1,239 of 1,564 at 30 s (225). The settlement projection in part 3b is the same calculation with h equal to the seconds remaining.

Observer-visible delay (part 4): because the spot value stamped s first enters the TWAP stamped s+3, the receipt-clock delay is measured for every second by pairing the two receipt clocks, no jump detection needed. Pilot week: Chainlink spot to TWAP median 3,088 ms (p10 2,552, p90 3,611, p99 4,042; 9 negative pairs of 550,393 are clock anomalies to list). Binance spot to TWAP median 5,707 ms when Chainlink picks the move up in the same stamp second and 6,707 ms when in the next. Decomposition, whole history: TWAP publisher stamp 1,399 ms after its observation stamp and receipt 375 ms after that; Chainlink spot 1,323 ms and 374 ms; Binance receipt 37 ms after its event time. Sub-second onset check on 771 single-second jumps of 3 bps or more: median share entering at s+3 is 1.00 (f3 median 0.000, f4 median 0.000), but per-jump dispersion is about plus or minus 1.2 jump units, so individual jumps cannot resolve a sub-second split and a jump-based delay estimator would inherit that noise.

Missing-input policy (part 3): in seconds with no retained Chainlink spot sample, carrying the last price forward reproduces the TWAP far better than averaging only the retained samples. On the 505,308 incomplete windows of the pilot week the carry-forward residual has median 0.0032 bps and 99th percentile 0.20 bps, against 0.0202 and 0.33 bps for the retained-sample mean; complete windows are identical under both (median 0.0000). Missing seconds are mostly isolated (23,971 single-second runs, 25 runs of 10 s or more in the week). Section 13 therefore rebuilds the week projection with carry-forward inputs and no market exclusions (2,016 markets per checkpoint); it supersedes the imputation used in part 2 section 10. A first formulation of section 13 that joined against whole-week per-second series was terminated after 10 minutes without output; the per-market formulation in part 3b replaced it. The whole-week series queries in part 3 loaded the 1 vCPU droplet noticeably; prefer the per-market pattern for future runs.

Sustained-step detector (section 3): Chainlink spot moves at least 2 bps over 2 s, then stays within 1.5 bps of the new level for 62 s, with 65 forward samples present; no explicit cooldown, no flat-prehistory test. Horizons are labelled in the section header.

Sample-key caveat for Binance comparisons (sections 4, 5, 15): the Binance spot collector keys each row by the wall-clock second at which it samples its in-memory latest price, so the row keyed s holds a ticker message received about 0.95 s earlier (received_ms minus sample_second_ms: p10 -955, p50 -948, p90 -944 ms in the pilot week). Chainlink spot rows are keyed by their whole-second provider stamp and received about 1.67 s after it. A same-key comparison therefore pairs a Binance price observed at real time s - 0.95 s with a Chainlink observation stamped s: "81% reflected in the same key second" means Chainlink reflects the move about one second later in real time, and 92% about two seconds later. Receipt-to-receipt delays in section 15 are unaffected by keying.
